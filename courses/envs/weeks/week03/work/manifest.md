- ⭐ Quiz flag: Three numbered problems with GNI per capita as a measure (slide-enumerated): (1) formal vs. informal economy, (2) variations within a country, (3) differences in cost of living. [L6]

- ⭐ Quiz flag: HDI defined as combining income + life expectancy + years of schooling; "a general association exists between HDI and GNI pc" but they are not the same measure. [L6]

## 4. Off-slide content

### Lecture 5
- Canada's "have" vs. "have-not" provinces example (Atlantic provinces receiving federal redistribution from Ontario/Quebec/Alberta) — illustrates sub-national spatial variation. [L5]
- Neighborhood-scale analogy (richer vs. poorer neighborhoods within the same city). [L5]
- Hans Rosling BBC video narrative detail: breaking China down into provinces/cities (Shanghai ≈ Italy's wealth/health; Guizhou ≈ Pakistan; rural Guizhou ≈ Ghana) to show within-country inequality. [L5]
- India–Britain cotton example for dependency theory: India was required to sell cotton exclusively to Britain, fueling the British industrial revolution. [L5]
- Critique of dependency theory raised informally: the US and Canada were also former British colonies but became wealthy — professor floats (without resolving) that racial dynamics might explain the difference. [L5]

### Lecture 6
- Concrete formal/informal economy examples: paying a friend cash to build a deck/shed, or paying someone cash to clean the house — contrasted with DRC, where up to ~80% of activity may be informal. [L6]
- Internal-inequality example naming Elon Musk, Warren Buffett, Bill Gates, Jeff Bezos vs. homeless individuals in the US, to illustrate why GNI per capita hides within-country disparity. [L6]
- Worked PPP milk example narrated beyond the slide numbers: framed as "10x richer by income, but only 4x richer by purchasing power" — the interpretive takeaway, not just the raw table. [L6]
- Note (not on slide) that in the PPP-adjusted map, Argentina and Chile move up into higher income categories relative to the raw-GNI map. [L6]
- Big Mac Index (The Economist) named explicitly as another PPP-style tool — slide shows only an image of an oversized burger, without the name. [L6]
- GapMinder video narrative detail: population blocks of 10 million shown at <$1/day, ~$10/day, and >$100/day income levels, reinforcing the "most people are in between" point. [L6]
- Dollar Street toothbrush example narrated in detail: finger-brushing in Myanmar ($47/mo) → shared family toothbrush in Cameroon ($174/mo) → individual toothbrushes in Palestine ($624/mo) → multiple/electric toothbrushes in France ($3,764/mo). Dollar figures are on the slide; the narrative framing is spoken only. [L6]
